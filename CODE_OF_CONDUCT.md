# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. This is a photo frame, not a courtroom.

## Our pledge

We want taking part in Star — reporting a bug, asking a question, sending a
patch — to be free of harassment for everyone, regardless of age, body size,
visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity
and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

## What is expected

- Be patient with people who know less than you do, and with people who know
  more. Both are a chance to learn something.
- Critique code, not people. "This leaks a handle on reconnect" is useful.
  "This is amateur work" is not.
- Accept that the maintainer may decline your change, and that this is not a
  judgement of you.
- Assume the other person is doing their best in a language that may not be
  their first.

## What is not acceptable

- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention
- Insults, personal or political attacks, and trolling
- Harassment, public or private
- Publishing other people's private information — a physical or email address,
  for example — without their explicit permission
- Anything else that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages — and whenever someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour is a problem, report it privately to the maintainer
rather than arguing in a thread. Use GitHub's
[report abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) form, which reaches both
GitHub and the maintainer, or send a direct message to
[@AlexRoman777](https://github.com/AlexRoman777).

Reports will be reviewed as promptly as a one-person project allows, and the
privacy of the reporter will be respected. The maintainer is obliged to say
plainly if a conflict of interest means they should not be the one handling it.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and
pull requests that do not follow this document, and will explain why when doing
so. Depending on severity, responses range from a private word, through a
temporary ban from project spaces, to a permanent ban.
